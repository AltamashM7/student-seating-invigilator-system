# Evaluation walkthrough

1. Open the application root: the student portal opens first. Use **Staff login** in the corner to sign in as admin/Admin@123. Dashboard: 2026-27, 240 students, 3 departments, 4 study years, divisions A/B. Each department-year has 20 students and each division 10; multiple enrollments do not inflate counts. Navigation is grouped and uses a menu button on narrow screens.
2. Academic Years creates calendar years; Divisions defines groups for the selected year. Students places a student in a division/semester. Promoting into another year retains the old placement. Select the year at the top to inspect its counts.
3. Courses captures study year, semester and department, and offers the catalog course in the selected year. Rows support edit/delete and offer/remove offering. Referenced records display protection messages.
4. Rooms creates numbered seats from capacity and columns per row. Configure accessible entry and individual seats before assigning the room to an exam.
5. CSV Import: download the evaluator sample from the page or select `database/evaluator-import.csv`. Preview confirms 24 rows and 9 columns, showing six example rows. On the clean demo it reports **12 new students, 12 additional enrollments, 0 duplicates, 0 invalid rows**; repeat import reports 24 duplicates. Each student joins CSE103 and CSE104, unpublished workshops that preserve existing seating. `database/student_import_template.csv` remains the separate format example. Enrollments also supports individual CRUD.
6. Exams selects an annual offering, date/time and room checkboxes. Room search and selected-capacity feedback simplify selection. Choose horizontal separation only with sufficient eligible capacity. Overlapping rooms/students are rejected. Drafts can be edited before seating history; publish to SCHEDULED for public visibility. **View seating** opens the selected chart directly.
7. Seating: choose CSE102, the prepared draft. It assigns 20 students to odd columns and marks accessible seats. Reassign a regular student to an empty eligible seat with a reason. Verify the manual marker and history. An even-column move or inaccessible move for an accommodation student is rejected without changing seating.
8. Swap occupied eligible seats; regenerate with full replacement unchecked to preserve both manual seats. Full replacement needs its checkbox and confirmation. History tracks the changes. Completed exams are read-only.
9. Student view is the default landing page; logout returns there. Search seating using `CSE26Y1A001`, 2026-27: Atharva Kulkarni's CSE101 appears at R101 / S001; CSE102 remains draft and is hidden. Search upcoming exams, open the full timetable, switch between list/date views and print seating details.
10. Faculty example: cse.faculty1/Faculty@123. Role access is restricted; duty allocation belongs to later sprints. Admin Accounts supports administrator creation and administrator-assisted recovery.

Management lists provide search, pagination and sorting; Students, Courses and Divisions also have department/study-year filters. **Add** opens the editor; row **Actions** contains editing/deletion. Seating search dims nonmatches without removing physical seats. Clicking an occupied seat selects its student; a target and reason are still required to save. Advanced controls remain collapsed until needed.

The fictional dataset has 240 students, 26 courses (including two import workshops), 24 divisions, 12 faculty, 8 rooms, 240 physical seats, 480 enrollments and 24 exams. `prepare-demo.py` creates 480 assignments and schedules 23 exams, leaving CSE102 draft. There are 24 accommodation students, with 48 subject assignments. Physical seats are reused across non-overlapping exams. Fixed dates are 12-23 October 2026. Names are fictional examples; email addresses use example.test.

Restore after demonstrations with `reset-local-demo.ps1 -ConfirmDisposable`, then `prepare-demo.py --confirm-demo`. Reset verifies this workspace's isolated MySQL data directory before replacing the demo; it does not reset the host database.
